# PrivacyRadar Deferred Work

These items were considered and intentionally deferred. They are not accidental omissions.

## Browser extension

What: show PrivacyRadar context at signup or while using a covered service.

Why: closer to the consumer’s decision moment than a destination website.

Depends on: proven demand for “check before signup/use,” stable public evidence API, browser permission/privacy review, and extension distribution support.

## Guided rights requests

What: downloadable or guided templates for access, deletion, correction, and opt-out requests.

Why: converts information into action without initially storing identity documents or acting as an authorized agent.

Depends on: validated action-card demand, region model, legal review, and maintained destination/contact data.

## Automated rights requests

What: submit and track requests on a user’s behalf.

Why: can remove substantial consumer friction.

Depends on: identity verification, authorization records, jurisdictional workflows, sensitive-data security, support, and liability analysis. Separate product initiative.

## Additional evidence layers

What: compare disclosures with app-store labels, permissions, trackers/SDK observations, breach history, and regulator findings.

Why: reveals discrepancies and is more defensible than policy summarization alone.

Depends on: core disclosure pipeline, source provenance model, methodology, and evidence-type-specific validation.

## Additional document types

What: Terms of Service, AI policies, cookie policies, DPA/subprocessor lists.

Why: important consumer changes may appear outside the privacy policy.

Depends on: stable multi-source company model, document-specific taxonomies, and action relevance.

## Additional notification channels

What: push, browser, SMS, messaging, or webhook delivery.

Why: users may prefer channels other than email.

Depends on: proven email retention, preference model, channel-specific abuse/security controls, and willingness to pay.

## Native applications

What: iOS and Android apps.

Why: notification and service-inventory experiences could improve on mobile.

Depends on: durable consumer retention that cannot be served by responsive web/email.

## Community features

What: comments, ratings, contributed interpretations, and public voting beyond coverage nominations.

Why: could improve discovery and corpus coverage.

Depends on: moderation, reputation, abuse prevention, editorial accountability, and legal policy. Currently rejected because it weakens trust.

## Full billing implementation

What: Stripe subscriptions, customer portal, invoices, failed-payment recovery, cancellations, refunds, and tax handling.

Why: funds continued monitoring once recurring value is proven.

Depends on: the retention, alert-to-action, allowance pressure, and willingness-to-pay gates in Milestone 7.
